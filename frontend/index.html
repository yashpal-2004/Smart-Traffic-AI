FIELD GUIDE

How the agent learns

A transparent view of the simulation mechanics and the limits of this model.

01 / ENVIRONMENT

A small, reproducible world

Four approaches feed one intersection. At each one-second step, vehicles arrive according to the selected scenario. A green phase can serve one vehicle per second from each of its two opposing approaches.

02 / STATE

What the agent observes

The Q-learning agent sees queue-length buckets for north, east, south, and west, the active phase, and a bucket for how long that phase has been green.

03 / ACTIONS

Hold, switch, or choose a green duration

The hold/switch agent chooses between staying green and requesting a change. The duration agent chooses a target green of 10, 15, 20, 25, or 30 seconds, then learns from the accumulated return over that whole interval. A minimum green time and amber transition constrain the signal safely within this simplified model.

04 / REWARD

Reward lower delay

The hold/switch learner gets a small throughput bonus and a queue-length penalty each second. The duration learner accumulates queue penalties across a full green interval and discounts the next decision by interval length. These are experimental reward choices; the duration policy has improved on hold/switch in our current benchmark, but it still trails fixed-time.

Model limitations

This is a toy single-lane model with Bernoulli arrivals and simplified movement. Its results do not represent real roads, pedestrians, turning traffic, safety requirements, or deployed signal systems.
